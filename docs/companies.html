Targets

Where to go deeper, by company

The core plan is the same everywhere. These notes say where each target pushes harder at staff level.

Based on how these companies describe their data stacks publicly and on common patterns in candidate reports. Loops change by team and level: read recent reports for your exact role before the onsite.

Google

Coding rigour close to software engineering loops, and designs at very large scale. The Dataflow/Beam model (event time, windows, watermarks) came from Google.

Go deeper on: Hard questions in P1 DSA topics, graphs, design-a-structure; S3 windows and watermarks. Cases: Ad-click aggregation, Clickstream analytics, Trending top-K.

Uber

Real-time data at very large scale. Uber built Apache Hudi and writes publicly about Kafka, Flink, Pinot and Presto.

Go deeper on: S3 stream processing, S9 real-time OLAP, late and out-of-order events, geospatial bucketing. Cases: Real-time trip and surge metrics, Streaming join of orders and payments, Feature store, CDC into a lakehouse.

Visa

Payments data: correctness, reconciliation, security and compliance come before speed.

Go deeper on: exactly-once and reconciliation, S11 governance and PII, auditability, S13 data SLOs. Cases: Payments transaction data platform, Real-time fraud feature pipeline, GDPR deletion, Customer 360.

Databricks

The Spark and Delta Lake company. Coding bars are often reported as higher than typical DE loops.

Go deeper on: S2 Spark internals, S4 table formats and the Delta transaction log; harder DSA. Cases: Transaction log for a lakehouse table format, CDC into a lakehouse, Hadoop to lakehouse migration.
