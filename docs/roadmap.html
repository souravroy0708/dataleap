From ten minutes a day to a staff offer

System design first, DSA second. Start so small you cannot fail, earn each level with complete days, and arrive at interview pace with the habit already built. Plan on about 24 weeks at full pace, alongside a full-time job.

The plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2Level 1, show up
    • 10 minutes of system design, 10 minutes of DSA. That is the whole day.
    • System design: short videos on distributed systems basics (S1): partitioning, replication, CAP.
    • DSA: Easy questions only. Reading the solution is allowed.
    • Write your why and set reminders on the phone calendar.

    Unlocks level 2 after 10 complete days.

  2. Weeks 3 to 4Level 2, build the habit
    • 20 minutes of system design, 15 of DSA.
    • System design: storage and formats (S4), then streaming (S3). Write a 5-line summary of every concept in your own words.
    • DSA: Easy into Medium, arrays, strings, sliding window, intervals.

    Unlocks level 3 after 14 complete days.

  3. Weeks 5 to 8Level 3, go deeper
    • 30 minutes of system design, 25 of DSA.
    • System design: design cases one part a day: requirements, estimates, architecture, data model, processing, failures, data quality, trade-offs. About one case a week.
    • Concepts continue: Spark and batch (S2), modelling (S5), CDC (S6).
    • DSA: Medium, 25-minute limit, out loud. Heaps, design-a-structure, data processing problems.

    Unlocks level 4 after 28 complete days.

  4. Weeks 9 to 20Level 4, interview pace
    • 45 minutes of system design, 30 of DSA on weekdays, more at weekends.
    • Two full design cases a week, 45 to 60 minutes, scored with the staff rubric. Drill your lowest score the next week.
    • Estimation and cost (S12), data quality (S8), orchestration (S7), serving (S9).
    • From week 14: one mock interview a week, alternating design and coding.

    Ready when the last five rubric scores average 10 or more out of 14 and Mediums take under 25 minutes.

  5. Weeks 21 to 24Apply and interview
    • Two mocks a week. Apply in batches so onsite loops land close together.
    • Keep the daily run going between loops, at level 2 pace if you need the energy elsewhere.

What I would do to crack a staff DE loop

  1. Turn your experience into design stories. Write up the 5 or 6 biggest systems you have built using the same 8-part case template, with real numbers, real incidents and what you would change. Staff interviews probe depth, and your years in production are the depth.
  2. Own the first 8 minutes. Ask about consumers, freshness, correctness and scale, then put numbers on the board before drawing a single box.
  3. Say trade-offs out loud. For every choice, name the alternative and what you give up. This is what separates staff from senior.
  4. Practise speaking, not just reading. Record one full design a week on your phone and watch 5 minutes of it.
  5. Score, then drill the weakest point. The rubric on every design case shows where you lose marks.
  6. Treat DSA as a gate. Mediums in Python in 25 minutes, data engineering problems, the revision queue. No need to chase Hard unless the target is Google.
  7. Get real mocks. Peers at product companies and paid mock platforms, from week 14.
  8. Protect sleep and family time. A burnt-out week breaks more streaks than a busy one.
