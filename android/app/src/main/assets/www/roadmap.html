The plan

From ten minutes a day to a staff offer

System design first, DSA second. Start so small you cannot fail, earn every level with complete days, and arrive at interview pace with the habit already built. About 24 weeks at full pace, alongside a full-time job.

The plan

  1. Weeks 1 to 2Level 1, show up
    • 10 minutes of system design and 10 of DSA. That is the whole day.
    • System design: distributed systems basics (S1): partitioning, replication, CAP.
    • DSA: Easy questions only, all tied to data work. Reading the solution is allowed.
    • Write your why and put reminders on your phone calendar.

    Unlocks level 2 after 10 complete days.

  2. Weeks 3 to 4Level 2, build the habit
    • 20 minutes of system design, 15 of DSA.
    • System design: storage and formats (S4), then streaming (S3). A 5-line summary of every concept, in your own words.
    • DSA: Easy into Medium on hashing, parsing, windows and intervals.

    Unlocks level 3 after 14 complete days.

  3. Weeks 5 to 8Level 3, go deeper
    • 30 minutes of system design, 25 of DSA.
    • Design cases one part a day: requirements, estimates, architecture, data model, processing, failures, data quality, trade-offs. About one case a week.
    • Concepts continue: Spark (S2), modelling (S5), CDC (S6), platform and staff judgement (S13).
    • DSA: Medium, 25-minute limit, out loud. Heaps, design-a-structure, data processing problems.

    Unlocks level 4 after 28 complete days.

  4. Weeks 9 to 20Level 4, interview pace
    • 45 minutes of system design and 30 of DSA on weekdays; more at weekends.
    • Two full design cases a week, 45 to 60 minutes each, scored with the staff rubric. Drill the lowest score the next week.
    • Estimation and cost (S12), data quality (S8), orchestration (S7), serving (S9).
    • From week 14: one mock interview a week, alternating design and coding.

    Ready when the last five rubric scores average 10 or more out of 14 and Mediums take under 25 minutes.

  5. Weeks 21 to 24Apply and interview
    • Two mocks a week. Apply in batches so onsite loops land close together.
    • Keep the daily run going between loops, at level 2 pace if you need the energy elsewhere.

What I would do to crack a staff DE loop

  1. Turn your experience into design storiesWrite up the 5 or 6 biggest systems you have built in the 8-part case template, with real numbers, real incidents and what you would change. Your years in production are the depth staff interviews look for.
  2. Own the first eight minutesConsumers, freshness, correctness, scale. Put numbers on the board before drawing a single box.
  3. Say every trade-off out loudFor each choice, name the alternative and what you give up. This is what separates staff from senior.
  4. Practise speaking, not just readingRecord one full design a week on your phone and watch five minutes of it.
  5. Score, then drill the weakest pointThe rubric on every design case shows exactly where you lose marks.
  6. Treat DSA as a gateMediums in Python in 25 minutes, data-shaped problems, the revision queue. Chase Hard only for Google.
  7. Get real mocks from week 14Peers at product companies and paid mock platforms. Real pressure, real feedback.
  8. Protect sleep and family timeA burnt-out week breaks more streaks than a busy one.
